# Footlights

Footlights is a Foundry VTT module for GM´s who want to do more than manage scenes. It helps you stage them. Instead of fighting tokens, UI windows, and improvisation stress during an important encounter, Footlights creates a clean stage: atmospheric backgrounds, fitting avatars, prepared sounds, and a stronger focus on: mood, drama, and shared imagination.

For campaign-heavy groups in particular, Footlights helps prepared moments actually land. You can enjoy scenes without distracting interface clutter, present them with more cinematic flair, and still keep the next important steps of the adventure close at hand.

## Why Footlights?

Footlights helps you

- make scenes feel present
- keep the group focused on the story rather than the Foundry interface
- give important NPCs and key moments more emotional weight through memorable avatars
- make atmosphere easier to feel through visuals, motion, and sound
- plan a campaign ahead of time without making live play feel rigid
- keep the GM oriented and flexible when players take unexpected turns

## Who Is Footlights For?

Footlights is especially strong for groups who

- care a lot about mood and storytelling

## Core Features

Before we take a look at the existing parts of this module, a small heads-up:
The module is not fully enclosed in itself, which means, you can drag foundry native elements into the planner for example (Tokens, Journals, Rolltables, etc.), or drag an actor from the stage goblin to a foundry native scene.

If you find something crucial missing, overseen or a cool feature that may be added, let me know!

### Cinematic Theatre of the Mind Scenes with Mood, Visual Identity, and Sound

Footlights gives you a presentation-focused stage for dialogue scenes, flashbacks, visions, dreams, interrogations, travel scenes, and dramatic reveals. The scene itself stays in the spotlight, not the technical scaffolding underneath it.
Each scene can have its own personality: background, subtitle, sense of place, mood setup, and matching sound playlists. That turns “you arrive at the tavern” into a moment with color, rhythm, and a distinct identity.

What this means for your group:

- less distraction from classic VTT elements
- a stronger sense of place, tone, and dramatic weight
- clearer attention on the speaking characters and the scene itself
- a great fit for groups who value immersion and narrative over tactical presentation
- immediate atmosphere as soon as a scene opens
- recurring locations and tones feel more consistent
- especially strong for intros, journeys, dreams, revelations, and boss moments

### Avatar Library for Important Characters

With a central avatar library, you can give NPCs, villains, factions, and the player characters a recognizable face. That makes callbacks easier, strengthens emotional memory, and helps important figures feel intentional instead of interchangeable.
Overall, the avatar library acts as a preconfigurable place for "Theatre of the Mind" Actors, offering customizable Avatars that appear during a Scene. This includes custom Frames and it also has the ability to give your Avatars/Characters "Moods". Either just per display as text or even setting up different Avatar Pictures for different moods, that are changeable while a scene is active. A villain is about to start to cry? Give him an image for it and make it visible!

What this means for your group:

- NPCs are easier to remember
- moods and attitudes come across faster
- recurring characters gain a stronger identity
- even short appearances feel more prepared and deliberate

### Sound Playlists for Music including a Soundboard for Atmosphere and Timing

Sound playlists let you prepare ambience, tension cues, and short audio accents in advance. You can then directly assign one or multiple playlists to a scene. This is especially useful when you do not want to explain emotion, but let the group feel it. 

What this means for your group:

- more atmosphere with less effort during play
- prepared sounds instead of frantic searching at the wrong moment
- stronger support for horror, mystery, travel, and epic reveals

### World Maps with Pins, Regions, Lines, Overlays, and Fog of War

Footlights also includes a world map library for large maps that would be difficult to use as one native Foundry image. Instead of asking the GM to manually prepare tile folders and metadata, Footlights can take a large source image, generate map tiles from it, and turn it into a zoomable, draggable map view.
This is meant for campaign maps, city maps, relationship maps, investigation boards, travel maps, faction territories, or any other visual space where the group benefits from being able to zoom from the big picture down to a specific location.

Maps can be opened in a regular window or in a fullscreen stage-style presentation mode. The fullscreen version can hide the Foundry UI similar to a theatre scene, giving the map its own focused stage while still allowing GM-controlled sidebars when needed.

What this means for your group:

- very large maps become usable without relying on one oversized image
- pins can mark settlements, points of interest, headquarters, clues, journals, or tokens
- categories let you show, hide, and lock related map elements together
- image and text overlays can add labels, symbols, faction marks, or custom visual notes
- regions and lines can outline districts, borders, routes, danger zones, or travel paths
- fog of war can cover unexplored areas and be revealed by the GM with brush or polygon tools
- maps can be started from the library, the Adventure Planner, the Stage Goblin, or macros
- GMs can force a map to open for players in windowed or fullscreen mode when the table should focus on it

What this means for your group:

## Example Footlights Workflow

A typical Footlights flow can look like this:

### 1. Create an Avatar

Start by creating an avatar for important characters. This gives major NPCs and recurring figures a clear visual identity right away.

### 2. Build a Scene

Next, create a scene with a fitting background, title, subtitle, and the selected avatars. Once the scene starts, it already feels like its own narrative space.

### 3. Add Sounds

Then combine matching sound playlists or short soundboard moments with the scene. That way, the mood is carried not only by the visuals, but also by the soundscape.

### 4. Plan in the Adventure Planner

Plan one or more of your prepared scenes in the adventure planner ahead. Connect important characters to places, make notes, assign rolltables and pin everything needed for fast play to the stage goblin bar. There you can easiliy sort your list.

### 5. Enjoy Theatre of the Mind

Once everything is prepared, you can let the scene play and focus fully on performance, dialogue, and tension. Footlights helps your group experience the moment instead of administrating it.

## Mini Tutorial: Adventure Planner

The Adventure Planner is built for GMs who want to do more than collect material. It helps you actively prepare the flow of an adventure or campaign.

### Quick Start

1. Create a new Adventure Planner for your adventure, arc, or chapter.
2. Add the most important building blocks: scenes, characters, notes, and likely plot beats.
3. Connect related elements so you can see what leads where at a glance.
4. Mark or pin the entries you are most likely to need in the next session.
5. Open the Stage Goblin and use it as your quick-access bar during play.

### How It Helps During Prep

The planner works especially well when you want to think ahead without locking yourself into a rigid script. Instead of forcing a fixed path, you can prepare stations, options, and relationships:

- Which scene opens the session?
- Which NPCs are likely to matter?
- Which clues point to which location?
- What escalates if the group takes a different route?
- Which scene or journal do you want instantly available?

### How It Helps at the Table

Pick the entries that matter right now and bring them into easy reach through the Stage Goblin. That way, you do not have to search through menus in the middle of a dramatic moment. You can just keep the session moving.

A simple practical approach:

- plan broadly in the Adventure Planner
- prepare only the most likely next steps for the upcoming session
- use the Stage Goblin as a short “what do I need next?” list

## Mini Tutorial: World Maps

World Maps are built for GMs who want a campaign-scale map without turning map preparation into a technical tile-management task.

### Quick Start

1. Create a new map in the Map Library.
2. Choose a large source image and, if needed, a thumbnail for the library card.
3. Click "Generate tiles from image" and let Footlights create the tiled map structure.
4. Open the map in windowed mode or fullscreen stage mode.
5. Add pins, labels, image overlays, regions, lines, and fog of war as needed.
6. Use category filters to show or hide whole groups of map elements during play.
7. Create macros or use the share buttons to open the map for players at the right moment.

### Preparing a Map

The intended basic workflow is simple:

- large image in
- Footlights cuts it into tiles
- the map becomes zoomable and playable
- the GM adds interactive information on top

This keeps the advanced tile setup mostly out of the way. You can still configure technical options like tile size, zoom limits, mouse-wheel zoom strength, overlay images, fullscreen behavior, and fog of war settings, but the normal GM flow should not require hand-written manifests or manual tile naming.

### Map Elements

A Footlights map can contain several kinds of information:

- Pins for places, journals, tokens, bases, clues, or points of interest
- Text overlays for names, district labels, warnings, or travel notes
- Image overlays for icons, symbols, faction markers, or custom graphics
- Regions for territories, danger zones, districts, or controlled areas
- Lines for roads, routes, borders, paths, supply lines, or connections
- Fog of war to hide unrevealed parts of the map from players

All of these can share the same category system. That means a category such as "Faction A", "Danger", "Discovered", or "Travel Route" can contain different element types and still be toggled together from the map legend.

### Using Maps During Play

Maps can be opened like other Footlights material from the library, from planner nodes, from the Stage Goblin, or from macros. When starting a map from planner or Stage Goblin, Footlights can ask whether the map should open as a normal window or in fullscreen mode.

The fullscreen mode is best when the map itself should become the table focus. The windowed mode is better when the GM wants to keep the map available while still working in the normal Foundry interface.
